## Review scope

All forty published selections, explanations, benefits and the previous questions were read. The revision adds distinct missing teachings and qualifications, then removes proposed restatements. Counts follow the material rather than a fixed target. The Hadith of Jibril receives 12 Q&A and 14 MCQs; the extended purity report receives 6 Q&A and 7 MCQs; drawing near to Allah receives 5 Q&A and 6 MCQs. Modesty and steadfastness do not receive artificially large banks merely to match these extended reports.

The 145 mapped points include the five pillars and six foundations, ihsan, decree and effort, the distinction between the Hour and its signs, sincerity, regular worship and expiation, charity and purification, the financial rights of the poor, heart and conduct, responsibilities toward others, graded correction of wrongdoing, major qualifications concerning hypocrisy, accountability for abuse, harmful accusations, and gratitude and patience. Every main clause in the longer selected texts is represented through connected explanation or practice. All source benefits and available vocabulary remain readable without turning each incidental descriptive detail into an isolated question.

## Source and interpretation safeguards

All 243 questions have exact publisher-source anchors and expandable supporting passages. An anchor validates cited wording, not every interpretation by itself; answers and mappings were also checked editorially against the source context. No new transmitted Arabic, English or Urdu wording is invented. The full original source content, IDs, references and narrator metadata remain unchanged.

The Jibril set retains both explained possibilities concerning the first sign and does not identify a country, person or timetable. The time of the Hour is distinguished from signs. Branch counts retain the transmitted alternatives and the publisher's nonexclusive explanation. The promise to Muadh is identified as grace, not an exemption from obedience. Allah's guidance of faculties is explained through guided conduct, and the closing death passage is treated within the published explanation.

The expiation report keeps the minor-sin and major-sin qualification. Unwanted thoughts are distinguished from accepted belief. The warning about regretful if concerns resentful objection, not all planning. Practical hypocrisy is not equated with concealed disbelief; failure despite sincere intention is not treated identically with deliberate promise-breaking. Religious accusations require verification and a proper purpose and manner. Intervention against wrongdoing retains knowledge, capacity and avoidance of greater wrong.

The existing context note for report 3055 remains: the occasion cited by the publisher is in another narration and is not part of this selected wording. The primary-reference note for 65018 also remains. Hakim's earlier good deeds after embracing Islam are distinguished from the separate report concerning dying without faith.
